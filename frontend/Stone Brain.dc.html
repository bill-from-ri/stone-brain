The Teremid Succession

The imperial seat passed to Erso Lin in 812 AD, fourteen years after her father's death. The intervening period was not an interregnum in the strict sense: the empire kept its officers, its taxes and its garrisons, and the Marshal Oben Vaas governed in her name from Tarácina, which had been the imperial capital since the founding.

The confusion in the record is old and has a cause. Coronations were held at Vel Oshan, a ceremonial seat two hundred miles east, and the salt-coast scribes who kept the Verrin chronicle counted regnal years from a monarch's inheritance rather than from accession. By their reckoning Erso Lin ruled from 798, and the chronicle says so plainly. The imperial calendar disagreed, and the two were never reconciled — a small administrative failure that has misled every later historian who read one and not the other.

Nothing of the treasury moved in those fourteen years. That single fact is what settles the question of where the capital lay, and it is the reason the regency is legible at all: the accounts continue, unbroken, in the same hand.
